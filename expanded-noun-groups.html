Expanded Noun Groups & Precise Description

Identify • Expand • Position • Compare • Apply

PAGE 1 • NOUN GROUPS

Build meaning around a noun

Key idea

A noun names a person, place, thing or idea. A noun group is a group of words built around a noun. The extra words help the reader know exactly which person, place, thing or idea the writer means.

thesmall silverrobot
determinerdescribing wordsmain noun

Worked example

robot is a noun. the small silver robot is an expanded noun group. The main noun is still robot.

Try it

What is the main noun in “those enormous gum trees”?

Think like a writer

Find the person, place, thing or idea first. Then notice which words have been added around it.

PAGE 2 • EXPAND

Add useful detail before the noun

Expand carefully

Writers can expand a noun group with words before the main noun. These can identify which one and describe qualities such as size, age, colour or number.

Build it step by step

bird→the bird→the young bird→the two young black birds

Each useful addition makes the meaning more specific.

Try it

Which is more precise: “a bag” or “the heavy blue school bag”?

PAGE 3 • ADD PHRASES

Add information after the noun

Key idea

A noun group can also be expanded after the main noun. A phrase can tell the reader more about location, association or another useful detail.

Compare

the seedlingsbeside the water tank

The main noun is seedlings. The words beside the water tank identify which seedlings are meant.

Another example

the old wooden bridge across the creek gives information before and after the main noun bridge.

Try it

In “the bicycle near the gate”, which words add information after bicycle?

PAGE 4 • PRECISION

Choose details that earn their place

More words are not always better

An effective noun group gives details that matter for the purpose. A scientific report, a story and an advertisement may describe the same thing differently because each text has a different purpose.

Informative purpose

the three juvenile green turtles on the eastern beach gives count, age, type and location—details that could matter in a wildlife report.

Imaginative purpose

the silent, shadowy path beneath the trees selects details that help create an atmosphere.

Try it

For a weather report, which is more useful: “the cloud” or “the dark storm cloud above the coast”?

PAGE 5 • APPLY & REASON

Use noun groups to improve a text

Informative writing

Basic: “Birds nested in trees.”

More precise: “Several rainbow lorikeets nested in the tall eucalyptus trees beside the oval.”

The added details identify the birds and the location more precisely.

Persuasive writing

Basic: “The park needs shade.”

More precise: “Our busy community park needs more large shade trees near the playground.”

The noun groups make the proposal more specific.

Reason and communicate

Ask: What does my reader need to know? Keep details that improve meaning. Remove details that are irrelevant, repetitive or confusing.

Try it

Expand “the shelter” so a reader knows which shelter you mean.
