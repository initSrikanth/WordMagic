PAGE 1 • COMPLETE SENTENCES

Express a complete idea

Key idea

A sentence expresses a complete idea. A clause contains a verb and usually has a subject: who or what the clause is about.

The curious magpiecarrieda silver ribbon.
subjectverbdetail

Worked example

Complete: “The storm passed.” It has a subject (the storm), a verb (passed) and expresses a complete idea.

Not complete: “Because the storm passed.” The word because makes the idea depend on another clause.

Try it

Which one can stand alone as a complete sentence?

Think like a writer

When you check a sentence, ask: “Can this idea stand by itself, or is it waiting for more information?”

PAGE 2 • CLAUSES

Main clauses and dependent clauses

Two useful clause types

Main clause

Can stand alone as a complete sentence.

The players cheered.
Dependent clause

Cannot stand alone in this context. It adds information to a main clause.

when the final whistle blew

Put them together

When the final whistle blew,the players cheered.

The main clause gives the central message. The dependent clause tells when it happened.

Try it

In “Although the path was muddy, we continued walking,” which clause can stand alone?

PAGE 3 • COMPLEX SENTENCES

Join ideas and show relationships

Key idea

A complex sentence contains a main clause and at least one dependent clause. Words such as because, although, when, if, before and after can show how the ideas are related.

Cause

We moved indoorsbecause the rain became heavy.

Because signals a reason.

Contrast

Although the task was difficult,Mina kept trying.

Although signals an unexpected contrast.

Try it

Which word best shows a condition: because, if, or although?

PAGE 4 • COHESION

Guide the reader from one idea to the next

What is cohesion?

Cohesion is the way parts of a text connect so the reader can follow the message. Writers can repeat key ideas carefully, use pronouns clearly, use connectives, and choose effective sentence or paragraph starting points.

Starting point matters

First, the class tested the soil.→After the test, they selected suitable plants.→Finally, the seedlings were planted.

The sentence openings guide the reader through the sequence.

Clear reference

“Asha placed the seedling in the hole. She gently pressed the soil around it.” The pronoun she clearly refers to Asha.

Try it

Which connective clearly signals a result: therefore, meanwhile, or for example?

PAGE 5 • APPLY & REASON

Choose structures for meaning and effect

Change the emphasis

Version A: “The team continued the match although the wind was strong.”

Version B: “Although the wind was strong, the team continued the match.”

Both express the same basic relationship, but Version B places the difficult condition first, giving it greater prominence.

Build a cohesive paragraph

First, the students measured the garden bed. Next, they calculated how many seedlings would fit. After that, they marked the planting positions. Finally, they planted the seedlings.

The openings make the sequence easy to follow without changing the events.

Reason and communicate

A good writer does more than join sentences. The writer chooses clause order, connectives and sentence openings to make relationships between ideas clear.

Try it

Complete the relationship: “Although the bus was late, ____.” Choose an idea that creates a clear contrast.
